MCNP–ISAP Showcases Health Research at Annual Health Research Forum 2026, Earns Student Recognition

MCNP–ISAP presented one 3-Minute Pitch to Policymakers and three digital poster entries at the Annual Health Research Forum 2026. Irish Ann Almida earned 2nd Best Paper, Student Category recognition.

Aligned SDGs:
RDC Agenda:

October 26–27, 2026 | Highlander Hotel and Resort, Solano, Nueva Vizcaya

The Medical Colleges of Northern Philippines (MCNP) and the International School of Asia and the Pacific (ISAP), through their engagement in research and development, participated in the Annual Health Research Forum 2026 held on October 26–27, 2026 at Highlander Hotel and Resort in Solano, Nueva Vizcaya.

Centered on the theme “Blending Research Innovation with Indigenous Knowledge, Culture and Resources for Sustainable Futures,” the forum provided a platform for research presentation, knowledge exchange, and engagement on health-related concerns and innovative approaches.

MCNP–ISAP participants contributed to the forum through a 3-Minute Pitch to Policymakers and digital poster presentations covering a range of health research topics.

John Carlo Suyu participated in the 3-Minute Pitch to Policymakers with “Rikna: Sa Mata Ng Mga Katutubo: Exploring Aeta's Concept About Mental Health.” The research brought attention to the concept of mental health from the perspective of the Aeta community.

Randolf Cabigas presented a digital poster on “The Social Stigma and Health Seeking Behavior of Community People Using Roy's Adaptation Theory,” addressing social stigma and health-seeking behavior within the community.

BS Pharmacy 4 students Irish Ann Almida and Kyle Angela Perucho presented “Formulation and Evaluation of Kalanchoe Pinnata (Kakataka) Young Leaves, Stems, and Roots Extract as a Cream for Antibacterial Properties.” The participation was highlighted by the recognition of Irish Ann Almida as 2nd Best Paper, Student Category, marking a documented achievement for MCNP–ISAP during the forum.

Rio Frances also participated through a digital poster presentation on “Physical Hazard and Its Relation to Body Mechanics and Musculoskeletal Health,” contributing another health-focused research study to the institution's participation in the event.

The forum strongly aligns with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 3 – Good Health and Well-Being, as the presented studies addressed mental health, health-seeking behavior, antibacterial properties, and musculoskeletal health. It also supports SDG 4 – Quality Education through research engagement and student participation and SDG 9 – Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure through research and innovation-oriented inquiry. SDG 17 – Partnerships for the Goals may also be relevant to the forum's collaborative research environment; however, no specific formal partnership involving MCNP–ISAP is claimed without additional documentation.

From a WURI perspective, the activity demonstrates potential relevance through research engagement addressing real-world health concerns, research innovation, and the presentation of research to broader audiences. The inclusion of a policymaker pitch also demonstrates an avenue for communicating research beyond conventional academic presentation. However, the available documentation does not establish adoption, implementation, scalability, or long-term outcomes of the presented research, so these should not yet be claimed as measurable WURI impacts.

The activity holds institutional significance by demonstrating MCNP–ISAP's participation in health research dissemination and providing opportunities for its researchers and students to present their work in a research forum. The recognition received in the student category further highlights the institution's engagement in developing and showcasing student research.

In terms of measurable results, the available documentation identifies one 3-Minute Pitch to Policymakers and three digital poster presentation entries, along with the documented 2nd Best Paper, Student Category recognition associated with Irish Ann Almida. No additional participant totals, evaluation results, research adoption data, or quantified community outcomes were provided.

While the Annual Health Research Forum is identified as a recurring event, the available information does not establish whether MCNP–ISAP's participation, the presented research projects, or related activities will continue or be expanded. Sustainability and longer-term research utilization therefore remain to be determined.

Through its participation in the Annual Health Research Forum 2026, MCNP–ISAP demonstrates its commitment to research engagement, knowledge exchange, and the advancement of health-related inquiry while providing avenues for researchers and students to contribute to discussions on health, innovation, indigenous knowledge, and sustainable development.

MCNP–ISAP Research and Development Center Develops Digital Platform to Improve Access to Research Information

The Research and Development Center (RDC) of the Medical Colleges of Northern Philippines (MCNP) and the International School of Asia and the Pacific (ISAP) has developed an institutional website to centralize research-related information, publications, resources, announcements, and institutional activities in one accessible online platform.

Aligned SDGs:
RDC Agenda:

The Research and Development Center (RDC) of the Medical Colleges of Northern Philippines (MCNP) and the International School of Asia and the Pacific (ISAP) has developed an institutional website to centralize research-related information, publications, resources, announcements, and institutional activities in one accessible online platform.

The initiative represents a step toward digital innovation in the presentation and dissemination of institutional research information. Through the website, students, faculty members, and other stakeholders can access information about the RDC's research initiatives, research resources, publications, news, events, and research opportunities.

By bringing these resources together in a centralized digital platform, the website aims to make research information more accessible and visible while supporting the RDC's institutional functions. It also provides a foundation for the continued development of online research information.

The development of the RDC website demonstrates an effort to use digital technology to improve the organization and accessibility of institutional information. Its potential benefits include more convenient access to research-related resources, improved online visibility of research activities, and opportunities to expand digital services as institutional needs evolve.

The initiative is primarily aligned with Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 9: Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure, through its use of digital infrastructure to support institutional research services. It may also contribute to SDG 16: Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions by supporting the public accessibility of institutional information.

In relation to the World University Rankings for Innovation (WURI), the website may be relevant to innovation in institutional practice and the potential scalability of digital services. As the platform develops, monitoring website usage, gathering user feedback, and documenting the number of resources published may help the institution evaluate its effectiveness and identify opportunities for improvement.

The development of the website reflects MCNP–ISAP RDC's initiative to strengthen its digital presence and provide a centralized point of access to institutional research information.

MCNP–ISAP Advances Statistical Literacy through Month-Long 37th Statistics Month Celebration

The Medical Colleges of Northern Philippines (MCNP) and the International School of Asia and the Pacific (ISAP) brought statistics beyond the classroom through a month-long celebration engaging all 17 academic departments in webinars, quiz bees, poster competitions, and infographic data interpretation.

Aligned SDGs:
RDC Agenda:

The Medical Colleges of Northern Philippines (MCNP) and the International School of Asia and the Pacific (ISAP) brought statistics beyond the classroom through a month-long celebration that combined training, creative application, competition, and inter-school engagement as part of the 37th Statistics Month Celebration.

Led by the Research and Development Center (RDC), the series of activities engaged all 17 academic departments of MCNP–ISAP and culminated in participation in provincial-level competitions involving 17 schools across Cagayan. The initiative aimed to strengthen students’ statistical competencies, deepen their appreciation of data, and provide practical opportunities to apply analytical and interpretive skills.

The celebration opened on September 15 with a Webinar on Infographics and Data Interpretation conducted via Zoom in collaboration with the College of Information Technology and Engineering. Students enrolled in statistics classes participated in the session, which served as preparation for the development of infographics from September 19 to 22. The activity emphasized how data can be transformed into clear, meaningful, and visually engaging information.

On September 21, students from different academic programs competed in the Institutional Quiz Bee at Amphitheatre 4. For ISAP, Fritz Geosh M. Apilado of BS Information Technology emerged as Champion, followed by Jaymart M. Gammad of BS Computer Engineering as 1st Runner-Up and Rhealyn B. Bitagon of BS Tourism Management as 2nd Runner-Up. For MCNP, Anjanet D. Tabunan of BS Nursing claimed the championship, while Marc Rodney G. San Pedro of BS Medical Laboratory Science and Chloe Rhayne M. Santos of BS Radiologic Technology placed first and second runners-up, respectively.

The celebration continued on September 23 with the Poster-Making Contest at Amphitheatre 3, where students demonstrated their ability to communicate statistical concepts through creative visual expression. Rhed Dalangin of BS Radiologic Technology won first place for MCNP, followed by Jireh Jaf Villanueva of BS Nursing and Ma. Jesusa Batacan of BS Pharmacy. For ISAP, Vince Ryan O. Obispado of BS Customs Administration took first place, with Mitz Invierno of BS Accountancy and Lyca Jeanne O. Paycao of BS Information Technology completing the top three.

Students were further challenged to interpret and communicate information during the Data Interpretation Using Infographics activity held on September 25 at the Instructional Media Center. Levi Mae Afali of BS Nursing emerged as Champion, while Mark Anthony Balbutin of BSSW and Cadiente Rolando of BSAC were named 1st and 2nd runners-up, respectively.

The institutional winners later represented MCNP–ISAP in the Provincial Quiz Bee and Poster-Making activities held on October 5 at Pulsar Hotel in Buntun, Tuguegarao City. The event brought together participants from 17 schools in Cagayan and provided students with a wider venue to test their competencies and engage with peers from other institutions. The MCNP–ISAP participants were coached by institutional statisticians Ms. Tshaine Anne Labang and Ms. Mary Jane Agabin.

Beyond competition, the Statistics Month celebration underscored the importance of statistical literacy in research, academic work, professional practice, and evidence-based decision-making. By combining lectures, infographic development, data interpretation, and competitive activities, the initiative provided students with experiential learning opportunities that required them to analyze information, communicate findings, and apply statistical knowledge in practical contexts.

The initiative directly supports Sustainable Development Goal 4 – Quality Education by strengthening students’ analytical and data literacy competencies through meaningful learning experiences. It also reflects SDG 17 – Partnerships for the Goals through the institution’s engagement with the Philippine Statistics Authority, while its emphasis on data interpretation, visualization, and applied quantitative thinking relates to SDG 9 – Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure.

The celebration also demonstrates WURI-relevant practices by moving statistical learning from theory to application. Its institution-wide participation, practical learning activities, partnership engagement, measurable reach, and potential for annual implementation make the initiative replicable, scalable, and sustainable.

The celebration likewise aligns with key directions of the RDC’s INNOVATES agenda. It supports INSPIRE Research Culture and Capability by strengthening students’ statistical literacy, analytical thinking, and research-related competencies; NAVIGATE Digital Innovation and Infrastructure through the use of infographics, data visualization, and digital presentation of information; OPERATIONALIZE Research Utilization and Innovation by translating statistical knowledge into practical applications through quiz bees, poster-making, infographic development, and data interpretation; EXPAND Partnerships and Global Research through collaboration with the Philippine Statistics Authority and participation in provincial-level activities involving other schools in Cagayan; and STRENGTHEN Education and Institutional Performance by integrating experiential, competitive, and application-based learning across all 17 academic departments.

Through the 37th Statistics Month Celebration, MCNP–ISAP continues to foster a culture where data are not simply computed, but understood, communicated, and used meaningfully—equipping students with competencies increasingly essential in research, innovation, and informed decision-making.

ISAP High School Students Strengthen Research Skills Through Research Defense

The International School of Asia and the Pacific (ISAP) provided high school students with an opportunity to demonstrate their research knowledge and develop essential academic competencies through its High School Research Defense, organized with the support of the Research and Development Center (RDC).

Aligned SDGs:
RDC Agenda:

The International School of Asia and the Pacific (ISAP) provided high school students with an opportunity to demonstrate their research knowledge and develop essential academic competencies through its High School Research Defense, organized with the support of the Research and Development Center (RDC).

The activity gave students a platform to present and defend their research topics before a panel, explain their ideas, and receive feedback to help improve their work. Through this academic exercise, students were encouraged to engage critically with their research and communicate their ideas clearly and confidently.

The research defense served as a practical learning experience that allowed students to demonstrate their understanding of their chosen topics. By responding to questions and considering the panel’s feedback, students had opportunities to strengthen their critical thinking, communication, and research skills.

Beyond the presentation of research topics, the activity highlighted the importance of research as part of students’ academic development. The defense process encouraged learners to examine their ideas, explain their reasoning, and consider recommendations for improving their research.

The initiative directly supports Sustainable Development Goal 4 – Quality Education by providing students with opportunities to develop research-related competencies, critical thinking, and effective communication through an applied learning experience.

The activity also demonstrates WURI-relevant educational practices by incorporating research presentation, panel engagement, and feedback into student learning. These practices provide a foundation for experiential and application-based education. Further documentation of student participation, research outputs, and subsequent improvements would help establish measurable outcomes and assess the activity’s potential for sustained implementation.

EVENT Research for the Goals

Pick a Goal. Raise Your Flag. Make Your Impact.

The SDG SPOT @ RDC: Research for the Goals officially came to life through its soft opening on August 28, 2026, in front of the Research and Development Center. Students, employees, and alumni joined by choosing an SDG to support, raising its flaglet, and capturing their commitment at the SDG Corner.

Read full story

More than a physical corner, SDG SPOT @ RDC is a year-round initiative of the Research and Development Center designed to bring the 17 Sustainable Development Goals closer to the academic community. It creates an interactive space where SDG awareness is connected with research, campus participation, practical action, and institutional improvement. Through the project, the RDC hopes to demonstrate that the Global Goals are not distant aspirations—they can be advanced through the studies we conduct, the ideas we share, and the actions we take within our own community.

The initiative primarily advances SDG 4 – Quality Education by strengthening SDG literacy, research awareness, and experiential learning. It also contributes to SDG 9 – Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure by encouraging research, solutions, and innovation; SDG 16 – Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions by providing avenues for stakeholder participation, student and employee voices, and evidence-informed institutional improvement; and SDG 17 – Partnerships for the Goals by bringing together students, employees, alumni, researchers, institutional offices, and potential partners in a shared commitment to sustainable development. While these four SDGs represent the project's strongest direct contributions, SDG SPOT @ RDC promotes awareness and engagement across all 17 Global Goals.

At the heart of the project are three interconnected activities:

GOAL MATCH

Pick a Study. Find its Goal. Discover its Impact. Participants connect actual research studies with the SDG they primarily address. By examining the purpose and potential contribution of a study, they discover how research can respond to concerns in health, education, environment, innovation, equality, governance, partnerships, and other areas of sustainable development. The activity also provides the RDC with a way to assess and strengthen understanding of research-to-SDG alignment.

SDG RESEARCH SNAP

Show Your Goal. Share Your Impact. Designed especially for student researchers, this activity makes SDG integration visible during proposal and final research defenses. Research groups identify the primary SDG connected to their study, complete the statement "I Research for SDG ___ because ___," and capture their research commitment with the corresponding SDG flaglets. Over time, these connections can provide a clearer picture of which Global Goals are being addressed by institutional research and which areas may need greater research attention.

SDG MISSION CARD

17 Goals. 17 Campus Actions. One Impact. This activity brings the SDGs into everyday campus life. Participants complete simple actions inspired by the 17 Goals—from sharing solutions to campus concerns and suggesting sustainability initiatives to expressing appreciation, promoting inclusion, and proposing innovations or partnerships. Their ideas and responses provide the RDC with stakeholder inputs that may reveal opportunities for research, innovation, sustainability initiatives, partnerships, and institutional improvement.

Together, these activities create one continuing journey:
UNDERSTAND the Goals
CONNECT them with Research
ACT within the Community
CREATE IMPACT.

The soft opening is only the beginning. As SDG SPOT @ RDC continues throughout the academic year, the initiative seeks to develop a community where students, employees, alumni, and researchers do not simply know the SDGs—they understand their role in advancing them.

Because the Global Goals become meaningful when they become our goals, our actions, and our shared impact.
